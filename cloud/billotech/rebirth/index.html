What the beat sounds like
  • Crying blues guitar up front — bends, slides, a blue note on the way down. That's the thread from the first record.
  • Church organ on a slow spin holding the chords, the way a Texas record sits.
  • 808 on a low E with slides into the turnaround. Kick locked to it.
  • Clap-and-snare backbeat, swung hats with triplet and double-time rolls.
  • Talkbox carries the hook melody where the sung hook goes.
  • The last four bars slow down and drop in pitch — screwed, on purpose.

What the two records share

The shared code
  • Same pocket. Both land in the low 80s BPM — the machine reads 83 on each.
  • Same move. Both ride a minor one-to-four vamp. Two chords, no rush.
  • Same weight. Over 60% of the energy lives under 250 Hz, under 8% above 2 kHz.
  • Near-mono. Side/mid width of 0.20 and 0.25. Built for cars, not headphones.
  • Loud. −8.7 and −7.5 LUFS.

Measured from the YouTube uploads, 150 seconds of each. Uploads can be pitched or re-encoded, so read tempo as ±2 BPM and key as ±1 semitone. Full four-way stem separation was not run; the read uses a harmonic/percussive split.
