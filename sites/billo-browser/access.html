Read the world. Don't pay per view.

Paywalls on news are mostly solvable with a library card, an archive, or the publisher's own free surface. This page is the ladder — the routes that actually hold.

1 · Your library card — the biggest unlock

Houston Public Library → WSJFree WSJ.com for 3 days, renewable from the same page. Covers digital print editions + company data. HPL databasesNYT Historical Archive 1851–2014, PressReader, NewsBank — Houston Chronicle included in HPL's news products. TexShareAny Texas library card → licensed databases remotely, incl. ProQuest Historical Newspapers. Find a participating libraryFort Bend County Libraries carry NYT; nearest branch lookup.

2 · Archives — for anything older

Wayback is the right tool for 2011–2016 press that no longer exists anywhere else.

3 · The publisher's own free surface

  1. Newsletter or RSS — often the full text, no meter.
  2. Syndication — search the exact headline in quotes; AP/Reuters/partner copies are often free.
  3. Free-article allowance — registering a free account is the intended path.
  4. The author's own copy — writers post PDFs to their site, Substack or LinkedIn.

4 · Academic & legal

Google Scholar “All versions” · Unpaywall · Open Access Button · arXiv · SSRN · DOAJ · CourtListener/RECAP for federal dockets · Justia for case law.

5 · Just ask

Email the reporter with what you're researching. As a working comedian and producer, press/industry rates for trade outlets are one email away.

6 · What we don't do

No meter resets, no bot user-agent spoofing, no paywall proxies. Those defeat access controls on copyrighted work, breach the publisher's terms, and fail unpredictably. The ladder above gets the same text and keeps your name clean.
